# Review of proposals stopped by VM admission

Every documented candidate family previously stopped by VM admission is included
in this comparison, together with all 22 items in the previous closing ledger.
Historical rejection and unavailable labels are inputs to review, rather than
reasons to remove a candidate before comparison. Each row separates the stopping
phase, the chosen rule, the actual operation reached and the later outcome.

This is an offline comparison of 16 documented candidate families, all 22 items
in the previous closing ledger, and selected original private receipts. No new
console observation, allocation, launch, GPU operation, CI run, admission change
or production adoption occurred. The [numeric ledger](vm-admission-review-20261027.json)
records source hashes, distinct refusal stages, later outcomes and all 22 mappings.
Its coverage is the repository's documented proposals and the named original
receipts; individual historical private cases outside those receipts remain
represented by their existing published evidence.

successes under separately declared scopes, not successful execution of a refused
case or proof that every title works at either value. They establish neither a
minimum nor repeatable startup at the later 3,402-page / 53.16 MiB observation.

The newer resident test's 6,396-page / 99.94 MiB screen is also a selected policy:
`4,986 prior global CPU decline + 1,154 operational floor + 256 variation`.
Its actual screen 3350 observed 5,049 pages and launched nothing. The later
3,402-page sample belongs to CPU census 3360, not to that refused screen.
The 4,986-page decline is not exclusive application consumption, a continuous
peak measurement or a measured bound for the new smaller title.

The comparison also separates the size of the margin from the underlying cost
estimate. Removing only the resident screen's 256-page variation changes 99.94
to 95.94 MiB; it does not validate the assumed 77.91 MiB CPU cost. Historical
full-title global CPU declines were 6,749 pages (105.45 MiB) and 4,986 pages
(77.91 MiB), across different titles and OS intervals. That difference is an
attribution question, rather than 27.55 MiB of proven application savings.
Case 3314 subsequently completed inference after an after-context observation of
1,415 pages, below its earlier 2,974-page startup target and above its 1,154-page
operational rule. These are distinct phase policies, not one universal requirement.

## Concrete accounting issue found in the earlier CPU comparisons

The original logs and actual helper source identify an unmeasured cross-pool
charge, rather than an allocation failure:

| Case | Actual VM free pages | Direct payload | Added VM requirement | Total selected VM requirement | Allocation API reached |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 2285 | 2,008 | 20 MiB | 1,280 pages / 20 MiB | 2,434 pages | No |
| 2288 | 1,761 | 10 MiB | 640 pages / 10 MiB | 1,794 pages | No |

Both had more free pages than the 1,154-page operational formula. The code added
the entire Direct payload as a VM prerequisite. Direct mappings can consume
OS metadata, but neither log measured that overhead or established a one-for-one
coefficient. This is evidence for correcting the accounting model, not evidence
that mapping overhead is zero or a Direct allocation would certainly succeed.

The actual sites are `demand_batch_probe.c::headroom` and its
`headroom(WINDOW/ALIGNMENT)` caller, immediately before
`sceKernelAllocateDirectMemory`. The same payload-to-VM pattern appears in
`bf16_palette_probe.c`, `completion_source_probe.c`, `vector_delivery_probe.c`,
`vector_io_small_probe.c`, `vector_pair_delivery_probe.c` and
`vector_layout_probe.c`, all under `native/qwen_flash_next/`.
`tools/completion_delivery_result.py` also requires
`additional_pages == total_Direct_bytes / 16384`; a revised experiment must update
its evidence interpretation together with the helper. The numeric ledger records
the exact seven file hashes and line sites plus both original journal hashes.
The full inference engine and the 99.94 MiB outer screen use different accounting;
this finding does not imply that every VM guard contains the same formula.

## Full backlog comparison, including additional blockers

The 22-item closing ledger also contains missing integrations and unsupported
interfaces, all retained in the numeric comparison. Width four/adaptive depth depend on width-two rejection/cost evidence;
actual hidden-state forecasting needs an implemented model tap and measured
usefulness. Further cache capacities, broader workloads and kernel profiling need
their own scopes. Radiance-derived dual-output/GDN/small-GEMM/workgroup candidates
still lack the indicated portable implementation or eligible cost measurement.
Their implementation, dependency and memory requirements receive separate
columns rather than a shared VM-unavailable label. The tested AIO path's measured
regression and the missing proprietary filesystem/raw-NVMe interfaces are likewise
included as separate outcomes in the OS I/O comparison.

CPU-helper Direct-size zero is also an observation-scope limitation, not evidence
that the native-title Direct pool is physically empty. Direct payload bytes,
Flexible availability, VM free pages and virtual reservations must stay separate.

## Admission review and next comparisons

The old records remain intact. The following interpretation supersedes their use
as permanent VM-feasibility decisions:

1. Compare an outer prelaunch estimate, a native pre-GPU target, a live-work
   predicate, a returned allocation error and unknown completion as separate
   outcomes. The audited selected receipts contain guard refusals, rather than
   an `ENOMEM` or Vulkan out-of-memory failure, for the still-unrun model candidates.
2. Treat 10,142 / 8,805 / 8,159 / 6,396-page estimates and the 2,974-page startup
   target as scoped policies. The 1,154-page operational formula also contains a
   chosen margin; it is not a demonstrated physical minimum. This review changes
   no guard and grants no allocation or GPU admission.
3. Review the seven helper cross-pool charges and the paired result verifier,
   pricing actual Direct availability separately from VM mapping overhead.
   Review stage-specific cost for the already built resident test, preserving
   original weights, fresh actual pool observations, bounded ownership, numerical
   checks and positive completion. Its 1,843 -> 1,693 load-image-page reduction is
   a static result, not 150 pages of measured VM recovery. A new lower policy
   requires that distinct scope and a bounded early-exit path, not a threshold
   chosen to fit the latest free-page sample.
4. Prioritize width-two rejection/restore and predictive off/on comparisons using
   the prepared single-load scope, followed by 53/128 slots and exact-prefix reuse
   in complete matched scopes. All historical rows remain in the comparison.
   Fresh execution decisions should name a changed scope or an unanswered metric
   and carry forward completed evidence instead of resuming unmatched old arms.
5. Rank PLE, ready-overlap, palette and demand refill using their actual numerical,
   timing and variability evidence, alongside the still-unmeasured candidates.
   In particular, the refill fixture's approximately 4% positive median deserves
   its own recorded status: the old 5% adoption threshold is a selected rule,
   rather than proof of zero benefit. Price any confirmation by its unanswered
   production-path question and preserve all added I/O/compute costs.

There is no new speed winner, stable-startup proof, memory recovery claim or
production change. Restart is not a prerequisite. The narrower native validations
and the original speed objective remain incomplete.
